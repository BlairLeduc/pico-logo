# Bugs

Tracks defects in Pico Logo — past, present and future. A **bug** here is
behaviour that contradicts
[`reference/Pico_Logo_Reference.md`](../reference/Pico_Logo_Reference.md),
silently loses or corrupts data, or hangs/crashes the interpreter.

Sibling document: [`roadmap.md`](roadmap.md) tracks
*features* — new primitives, designs, milestones and performance work. If an
item adds capability it belongs there; if it means "this doesn't work as
documented", it belongs here.

**Status legend:** `open` · `in progress` · `fixed` · `won't fix`

**Severity:** `high` (data loss, crash, hang, unusable feature) · `medium`
(surprising wrong behaviour with a workaround) · `low` (cosmetic or narrow
edge case)

### B19 — Collision tests ignore the wrapped playfield

Every box test in `logo/games/asteroids` compares raw coordinates, so two
objects three steps apart *across* an edge read as 317 steps apart and never
collide. Found while instrumenting B18: a ship at x = 158 and a saucer shot at
x = −159 pass through each other.

The playfield wraps for **drawing** (the engine's `wrap` splits a segment across
the edge) and for **motion** (`wrapc` and `setspeed`), so the picture is right
and only the arithmetic is wrong. It affects every pair — shots on rocks, rocks
on the ship, the saucer on anything — in a band a few steps wide at each edge.

- **Status:** open, and not obviously worth fixing. The correction is a wrapped
  distance (`if d > 160 [make "d 320 - d]`) on both axes of every test, which is
  two comparisons and a subtraction **per pair per frame** — about 24 extra
  statements a frame at twelve rocks, against a frame that is already at its
  budget (the design's §12f). What it buys is correct behaviour in perhaps 5 %
  of the field, in a game where the objects that matter are usually near the
  middle. Worth revisiting if the collision pairs ever get cheaper.
- **Found:** 2026-08-12, while probing B18.

### B6 — `penreverse` ignores pen size (always 1 px)

`penreverse` toggles pixels, so overlapping thick stamps would toggle shared
pixels twice and speckle the line. Thick reverse drawing is therefore left at
1 px and documented as a limitation rather than implemented.

- **Status:** won't fix for now. A future thick-reverse pass would delta-stamp
  only the pixels the previous stamp did not cover, or fill per-scanline spans.
- **Found:** 2026-07-18, with `setpensize` / `pensize`.

| 2026-08-13 | Asteroids' small saucer ranged on the spawn point during a respawn (B22) | games | Reported from a board: killed by the small saucer, the player respawned into shots already aimed at them and died repeatedly with no chance to escape. **The in-flight shots were not the cause** — those track the place the player *died*, which is not the spawn point. The cause was that the saucer went on aiming for the whole death and the whole wait. `saucer.fires` reads the ship's **drawn** position, and `respawn` writes the spawn point into `shipx`/`shipy`, so from the instant the explosion ended the small saucer had a perfect firing solution on a stationary target that was about to materialise — at `sau.aim.tight` (4°) once the score is up, on a point that is identical between deaths, so it did not even have to re-range. The timing sealed it: `sau.fire.gap` is 17 frames and `death.frames` is 10, so a shot fired shortly before the kill arrived just after the ship landed, and `respawn` lands the ship with zero velocity and heading 0. **The arcade never has this state**: a destroyed ship there is a deactivated object with no position until a new one is created at the centre, so its aiming routine has nothing to read. What made it reachable here is the M3→M4 respawn trick — parking the waiting ship **on** the spawn point so the rock pass doubles as the clear-check — which is good for collision and leaked into the aiming, a value chosen to make one test cheap becoming an input to an unrelated one. Fixed by guarding the aim on `dying` and `waiting`: a small saucer with no target fires anywhere, exactly as the large one does. With the guard in, the drawn position is correct for every shot that is actually aimed, because the only two states where it differs from the collision position are the two now refused. `test_a_saucer_does_not_range_on_a_ship_that_is_not_there` pins all three cases (live ship aimed at 20/20; waiting and dying both scattered) and counts shots rather than judging one, since a 4° spread lands near the ship by luck often enough to pass a single-shot test. The old `test_a_saucer_aims_at_a_ship_it_cannot_yet_hit` asserted the defect and was replaced — its intent (never aim at the 9999 park) survives as the guard | |

| 2026-08-13 | Asteroids' heartbeat was a click and not a note (B21) | games | Reported from a board: *"the heartbeat sound is now clicks instead of being tones"* — and the game was making exactly the sound it asked for, so this is a tuning defect rather than an engine one. `setwave [0 4] "triangle` at **78 Hz**: a triangle's harmonics fall away as 1/n², so the third sits 19 dB down at 234 Hz and the fifth 28 dB down at 390, and the fundamental itself is far below where the PicoCalc's small speaker begins to move air. There was nothing in the note to hear. What there *was* to hear was its onset: the oscillators start every gate from phase 0 and `setenv [0 4] [0 90 0 70]` asked for a 0 ms attack, so each beat began with a full-scale step — the one waveform a small speaker reproduces perfectly. **The tempo was right and the note was inaudible, so what came out was a metronome of clicks.** Three changes, one per layer: the two notes go an **octave up at the same 6:5 interval** (156 and 130 Hz, exactly double 78 and 65, so they are still two low notes); the wave becomes a **square**, whose harmonics fall as 1/n and therefore carry a low fundamental through a small speaker, which is how chip music has audible bass at all; and the envelope becomes `[25 30 9 45]` — an attack that ramps instead of stepping, and a sustain, because a note that decays straight to zero has no steady part to hear as a pitch. **The number worth carrying away is 3.5 ms**: the engine advances envelopes once per refill block (`SOUND_RING_HALF`, one ring half) and `steps_for` clamps to one block, so *any* attack under 7 ms is a full-scale step regardless of the number asked for — 0 ms and the engine's own "click-free default" of 5 ms are bit-identical in effect. Anything shaping a note in this tree has to be written in multiples of 3.5 ms to mean anything, and that is now in [`asteroids-design.md`](asteroids-design.md) §11.2. Not reachable by a host test — the mock records the timbre but nobody hears it — so `test_the_timbres_are_set_once_and_match_the_voice_kinds` pins the wave and the level-end test pins the envelope, and the *sound* is a hardware verification. Present since M4 shipped the sound; §11 had flagged frequencies and volumes as the one part of the file no host test could judge, and this is what that flag was worth | |

The cause is geometry, not timing. The ship is a triangle 24 steps long: its **nose reaches 12 steps from the centre and its rear corners 12.73**, while its **beam is 9** — and `sau.shot.rad`, the half-width of the box a saucer shot kills the ship in, was **9**. | 2026-08-11 | Asteroids died with "Out of space in step.rock" within a minute of play | games | Reported from a board. `logo/games/asteroids` recycled every 250 frames, the interval all three shipped games use, and it is not transferable: their frames spend **zero** cells, and an Asteroids frame mints an atom for every rock position it stores — `.setitem` of a *number* interns it (`member_value_to_node`, `core/primitives_words_lists.c`), so twelve rocks cost 36 atoms a frame. ## Reporting a bug

1. Write a failing test first (`tests/test_<module>.c`, mock device) that
   reproduces it — see the Unit Testing rules in
   [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md).
2. Add a row to **Open** above with the next `B<n>` ID and a detail section.
3. Fix it, run `ctest --preset=tests`, then move the row to **Fixed** with the
   date and PR.

Bugs that cannot be reproduced on the host (real lwIP or cyw43 timing, PSRAM,
audio DMA) should say so explicitly and record how they were validated on
hardware instead.
